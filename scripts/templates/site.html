What is a JEPA?

A Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture learns representations by predicting in latent space. A context encoder embeds the visible part of the input, and a predictor estimates the embeddings of masked or future target regions produced by a target encoder (typically an EMA of the context encoder).

Because it never reconstructs pixels or raw signal values, a JEPA focuses on semantic structure and ignores low-level noise, which makes it a natural fit for medical data, where much of the raw variance is acquisition noise.

Visible context→Context encoder→Predictor→Predicted embeddings⇢ latent loss ⇠Target encoder (EMA)

How to read this list

  • Direct Medical JEPA: applies or extends a JEPA objective on medical or biomedical data.
  • JEPA-inspired adapts JEPA ideas without being a named JEPA, or benchmarks JEPA against other objectives.
  • Foundational JEPA: general-domain architectures and theory that medical JEPA work builds on.
  • Code links only to official author implementations. arXiv and bioRxiv venues are preprints.
